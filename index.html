The studio

Twelve looms, one street, no two lengths alike.

NŪL began in 2012 when three weaving families on the same lane in Coimbatore decided to sell under one name instead of competing for the same middlemen. Nūl means thread in Tamil — the studio is named for the only material that hasn't changed since.

Every saree, stole, and length of yardage passes through the same hands from warping to finishing. We keep runs small on purpose: a loom can only move as fast as the person sitting at it, and we've never wanted to change that.

We work almost entirely in cotton and cotton-silk blends, dyed with indigo, madder, and turmeric sourced from growers within the district.

The process

From raw thread to finished cloth, in four sittings.

Nothing here is automated. Each stage is done by a different pair of hands, in the order it has always been done.

01

Warping

Threads are measured and stretched across a warping drum — often over 3,000 ends for a single saree — set to the exact length of the finished cloth.

02

Weaving

The weaver throws the shuttle by hand, passing weft over and under the warp — roughly 4,000 throws for a metre of plain-weave cotton.

03

Natural dyeing

Undyed cloth is soaked in vats of indigo, madder root, or turmeric, then sun-dried on the terrace — a step that can take up to three days per colour.

04

Finishing

Loose ends are trimmed, borders checked by hand, and each piece is measured and pressed before it leaves the studio.
